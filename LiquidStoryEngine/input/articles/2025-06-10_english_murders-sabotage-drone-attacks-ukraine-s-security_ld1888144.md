# With murders, sabotage and drone attacks, Ukraine's security service stays step ahead of Russia

*Last week, Ukraine's security service shocked the world – and especially Moscow – by destroying vital military aircraft across Russia, following up days later with an attack on the Crimea bridge. Five operations in recent years shed light on how the agency operates successfully behind Russian lines.*

**Andreas Rüesch** · NZZ in English · 2025-06-10 · 1649 words · [ld.1888144](https://www.nzz.ch/english/behind-ukraines-secret-operations-and-sabotage-ld.1888144)

> Summary
> -  Ukraine's SBU security service has successfully carried out numerous operations deep inside Russia, including drone strikes on military bases, sabotage and assassinations.
> - The agency has access to a broad network of agents as well as considerable technical expertise, and sometimes uses unwitting Russian locals in its plans.
> - The actions have been aimed especially at weakening Russia's military infrastructure and logistics capabilities.

On Sunday, June 1, the Ukrainian Security Service, or SBU, mounted a devastating attack on military airfields far behind Russia's front lines, successfully destroying around a dozen critical aircraft. The intelligence service then waited less than two days before striking again. On Tuesday, June 3, it published a video of an explosion under the strategically important Kerch Bridge, which leads from mainland Russia to the occupied Crimea peninsula.

The bridge did not collapse, but this does not mean that Russia emerged unscathed. The aim of the underwater explosion was evidently to weaken the structure's supports. The pillars visible in the video are not anchored directly to the seabed, standing instead on a group of thinner struts. If some of these have been destroyed, the bridge may have been rendered unstable. That will become clear only over time.

This is the third time since 2022 that Ukraine has attacked this important supply route for the Russian military. In a statement posted on the Telegram messaging service, the SBU said it deployed agents who placed mines on the underwater pylons, which were subsequently detonated by remote control. Whether this is fiction or various were actually able to reach the well-protected bridge with explosive charges cannot as yet be confirmed.

In general, much remains mysterious about the workings of the Ukrainian security service, which carries out both counterintelligence and military functions. What appears certain is that the SBU has a large network of agents at its disposal, enabling it to carry out complex operations on Russian soil. Five initiatives in recent years have brought these capabilities into the spotlight.

## Attempted assassination of nationalist ideology

![Alexander Dugin in 2023.](https://img-msp-prod.nzz.ch/2025/06/03/604f5d1f-c368-499e-b4e3-e0a676ada1da.jpeg?width=1024)
*Alexander Dugin in 2023.* (Anadolu / Getty)

On Aug. 20, 2022, six months after Russia's invasion of Ukraine, a car exploded and burst into flames on a main street in Moscow. Daria Dugina, the daughter of prominent ultranationalist and was supporter Alexander Dugin, was behind the wheel. There are many indications that the attack was aimed at Dugin himself. He and his daughter were returning from one of his appearances that evening, and he is said to have decided at the last moment to use a different vehicle.

Ukraine initially denied any responsibility for the assassination, but sources within the SBU later admitted responsibility. Dugin is one of the most prominent ideologues of Russian power politics, and was thus a plausible target. Russian authorities later reported that a group of saboteurs had carried out the attack. They accused a Ukrainian man of constructing the explosive device in a Moscow garage, and a Ukrainian woman of planting the bomb under the car. Both were able to flee abroad, the authorities said. According to unofficial Ukrainian sources, the SBU brought the material for the bomb across the Russian border in a cat carrier.

## Assassination of military bloggers

![Russian military blogger Vladlen Tatarsky.](https://img-msp-prod.nzz.ch/2023/04/03/3f368f98-2927-4929-9297-c4925880a85a.jpeg?width=1024)
*Russian military blogger Vladlen Tatarsky.* (Telegram via Reuters)

A similar but more complicated operation was the killing of Russian military blogger Maxim Fomin, who wrote under the pen name Vladlen Tatarsky, on April 2, 2023. Tatarsky was an influential propagandist who strongly supported the war on his popular Telegram channel, calling for a campaign of relentless brutality against Ukraine. During one of his speaking engagements in St. Petersburg, a young woman who claimed to be a fan presented him with a statuette as a gift. It contained an explosive device, which detonated when Tatarsky examined the object. He was killed instantly. Numerous other audience members also suffered injuries.

SBU head Vasyl Maliuk later admitted responsibility for this attack as well . The security service used a woman from the Russian anti-war movement, who was probably unaware of the goal, to carry out the attack. The Russian woman, who was later sentenced to a long prison term, evidently received the statuette containing the explosive device from an agent who did not tell her about the deadly intent behind the plan.

## Deadly attacks on two high-ranking generals

![Russian investigators at the site of the bomb attack on Lt. Gen. Yaroslav Moskalik in April.](https://img-msp-prod.nzz.ch/2025/06/03/4ec6ea8d-28a2-4437-865b-799d3084937e.jpeg?width=1024)
*Russian investigators at the site of the bomb attack on Lt. Gen. Yaroslav Moskalik in April.* (Imago)

In December 2024 and April 2025, two high-ranking Russian military officials were killed in bomb attacks, respectively in Moscow and in a suburb of the capital. The operations were very similar. Both Lt. Gen. Igor Kirillov and Lt. Gen. Yaroslav Moskalik was killed in front of the entrances to their homes. In Kirillov's case, the remote-controlled explosive device was hidden in an electric scooter, and in the attack on Moskalik, it was hidden in a parked car.

The SBU publicly took credit for the attacks. It had clearly acquired detailed knowledge of the two military officers' living arrangements and habits, and it chose its victims carefully.

Kirillov was head of Russia's nuclear, chemical and biological weapons protection forces , and had commanded units that took part in the war against Ukraine. He had been accused of using chemical weapons in violation of international law. Moskalik was deputy head of the main operational department of the Russian armed forces' General Staff , and in this capacity he was closely involved in war planning.

## Explosion of truck bomb on the Kerch bridge

![The fire on the Kerch Bridge after the explosion in October 2022.](https://img-msp-prod.nzz.ch/2024/03/18/cb3a8ffe-9a4c-43bb-81f6-835f6ee1cad3.jpeg?crop=4527,3018,x9,y0)
*The fire on the Kerch Bridge after the explosion in October 2022.* (AP)

The bridge from the Russian mainland across the Kerch Strait to the Crimean Peninsula is a strategically important transport link. For Russia, it is also a symbol of its rule over Crimea, which it occupied in 2014. Moscow built the structure rapidly after annexing the peninsula, despite protests from Ukraine. With the start of Russia's large-scale war against its neighbor, the bridge immediately became a target for the Ukrainian security service.

On Oct. 8, 2022, a surveillance camera recorded a truck exploding on the bridge. The detonation hurled a section of the road into the sea. The huge fireball also sets a freight train on the parallel railway bridge on fire. Repairs to the road and rail links took seven months, during which time Russian logistics were severely hampered.

The SBU claimed responsibility for the attack only the following year, and also provided details of how it was carried out . The security service hid several tons of explosives wrapped in rolls of cellophane film, and sent the cargo on a complicated route via the South Caucasus to Russia. There, a local trucking company took over the transport to Crimea. The driver was killed in the explosion.

## Acts of sabotage against Russia's rail transport system

![A train that derailed in Russia's Bryansk region following an act of sabotage.](https://img-msp-prod.nzz.ch/2025/06/03/69f2704b-5829-4963-a930-a5a0a292c72c.jpeg?crop=3153,2102,x0,y0)
*A train that derailed in Russia's Bryansk region following an act of sabotage.* (Dmitry Radchenko / Imago)

During the course of the war, Ukrainians have targeted Russian railway lines with numerous acts of sabotage. In each case, the aim has been to disrupt military logistics. Some of these were attacks carried out by Ukrainian partisans in the occupied territories, while others were incidents in Russian regions close to the border. Recently, two explosions on the same day caused a stir when they brought down bridges in the border regions of Bryansk and Kursk, derailing trains.

However, the Ukrainians have in some cases also struck deep into Russian territory.
