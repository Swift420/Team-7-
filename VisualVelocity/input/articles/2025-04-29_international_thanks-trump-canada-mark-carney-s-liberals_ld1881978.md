# Thanks to Trump: In Canada, Mark Carney's Liberals defeat the Conservatives

*For a long time, it looked like a change of power was imminent. But after the American president alienated the Canadians, the Liberals won the parliamentary election and Carney remained Prime Minister.*

Canada's ruling Liberal Party won Monday's parliamentary election. However, it is now clear that they narrowly missed an absolute majority. At least 172 seats would have been needed for an absolute majority in parliament in the capital, Ottawa.

Mark Carney now needs the support of smaller parties – such as the social-democratic New Democratic Party (NDP), the regional party Bloc Québécois, or the Greens – to form a minority government. Minority governments in Canada typically last only a few years. However, in the current political landscape, it should be significantly easier for the Liberals to enter into a coalition with a smaller party than it would be for the Conservatives.

Both the Liberals and the Conservatives made significant gains compared to the last elections in 2021. These gains came primarily at the expense of the left-wing New Democratic Party (NDP), which lost more than ten percentage points. Its leader, Jagmeet Singh, also lost his seat in parliament, as did the Conservative leader, Pierre Poilievre.

## Trump brought about the change

The high voter turnout already indicated that this was a pivotal election. 7.3 million of the 29 million eligible voters cast their ballots before election day – a record high. Voter turnout, at 67 percent, was also higher than in the last election.

Indeed, the world's second-largest country by area had rarely experienced such a radical shift in public opinion in such a short time. Until three months ago, polls showed Poilievre's Conservatives clearly in the lead, with no chance for the Liberals to close the gap. But then the aggressive tariff policies and annexation threats of US President Donald Trump threw Canadian politics into disarray. They unexpectedly boosted the popularity of none other than Trump's opponent, the liberal economist Carney. Suddenly, the banker, who had no political experience whatsoever, was the man of the hour.

On election day itself, Trump posted on his network Truth Social that Canadians should vote for him. «Stay out of our elections,» Pierre Poilievre countered on X, and Carney replied, also on X: «This is Canada – we decide what happens here.»

Canadians rallied together in their resistance against Trump. A wave of patriotism swept through the country. Many are now boycotting American goods, no longer traveling to the USA, flying Canadian flags, and wearing caps that read «Canada is not for sale.»

## «Canada first» candidate versus experienced crisis manager

This made the election campaign difficult for the conservative candidate Poilievre, whose rhetoric was modeled on Trump's. His polarizing style suddenly seemed out of touch in light of the new unity. The 45-year-old, who campaigns for low taxes and cuts in government spending, repeatedly spoke of fake news, just like Trump, warned in apocalyptic tones against the woke ideology of radical left-wing forces, and promised to always put Canada first – «Canada first.» This approach resonated well for a long time – until Trump became president again and attacked Canada, its long-time close ally, in a way that no one would have thought possible.

«Canada’s old relationship with the United States, based on the intertwining of our economies and close security and military cooperation, is over,» Carney declared.

The liberal economic expert brings national and international crisis experience and is considered resilient. During the financial crisis, he headed the Bank of Canada from 2008. Canada's relatively successful weathering of the global turmoil at that time is also attributed to him.

Between 2013 and 2020, Carney served as Governor of the Bank of England during the chaotic Brexit period, and subsequently as UN Special Envoy for Climate Change until January of this year. He advocates for closer cooperation with Europe and Asia to reduce economic dependence on the US. He also wants to eliminate trade barriers between Canadian provinces to promote the single market. Although Poilievre attempted to portray his opponent Carney as a clone of Trudeau during the election campaign, he was unsuccessful. The election results confirm what polls had already clearly shown: most Canadians believe Carney is better equipped than Poilievre to stand up to Trump.

## Trudeau's departure

«I have handled crises before,» Carney repeatedly said during the election campaign. And, with a clear dig at the right-wing populist Poilievre and his provocative style: «This is a time that requires experience, not experiments.»

The Liberal Party has been in power in Canada, a G7 country with around 40 million inhabitants, for about ten years, initially with an absolute majority, then with a minority government, and until recently under the leadership of Justin Trudeau. He was long admired as a charismatic golden boy.

Faced with a weakening economy and rising prices, however, the pressure on him increased, and his popularity declined. At the beginning of the year, Trudeau announced his resignation. Following an internal party vote, Carney was elected the new party leader and thus automatically also Prime Minister. Shortly after his election, he called a snap election, thereby initiating the revival of the struggling Liberals. Now, strengthened and with the support of the population, Carney can enter the fight against Trump.

In his victory speech, Carney said, «America wants our land, our resources, our water.» He cautioned that these were not empty threats; rather, the aggressive tariff policies and annexation threats were interconnected: «President Trump is trying to break us so that America can own us—but that will never happen.» Carney is trying to look to the future with optimism, but it is clear how deeply Trump's actions have affected Canadians: «We have overcome the shock of American betrayal, but we should never forget the lessons.»
