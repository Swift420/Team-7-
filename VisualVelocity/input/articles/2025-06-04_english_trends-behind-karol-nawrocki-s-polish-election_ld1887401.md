# The trends behind Karol Nawrocki's Polish election win

*Young men and a bit of help from Donald Trump? National conservative Karol Nawrocki won a narrow victory in Sunday's presidential election. Maps and charts explain four key insights from the results.*

**Eike Hoppmann** · NZZ in English · 2025-06-04 · 686 words · [ld.1887401](https://www.nzz.ch/english/how-karol-nawrocki-won-the-polish-election-ld.1887401)

> Summary
> -  Karol Nawrocki narrowly won the Polish presidential election against Rafał Trzaskowski, with rural areas and younger men playing a crucial role.
> - Geographic and demographic analyses reveal an East-West divide: Nawrocki dominated in the country's southeast, while Trzaskowski led in cities and the northwest.
> - Polish voters abroad were also divided. Those in Europe largely supported Trzaskowski, while Nawrocki came out ahead in North America. Overall, voter turnout reached a record high of 71%.

It has never been so close: 1.8 percentage points, or the equivalent of just under 370,000 votes, decided the outcome of the presidential election in Poland . **Karol Nawrocki** , supported by the national conservative Law and Justice Party (PiS), prevailed over **Rafał Trzaskowski** , who belongs to Prime Minister Donald Tusk's liberal pro-European Civic Platform.

> [embed: q-tool (chart/map) 596f09f1501e5776fb09e4901bf89961]

Polish society seems to be split into two roughly equal camps. Data analysis of the election results shows what may have tipped the scales in Nawrocki's favor.

## The northwest and the cities voted for Trzaskowski, the rest for Nawrocki.

Two geographical patterns can be seen in the election results: First, Trzaskowski, who is the mayor of Warsaw, won in the capital and other urban areas. Nawrocki won over voters in rural areas.

> [embed: datawrapper chart ]

Second, there is an east-west divide. The national conservative PiS party has its strongholds in the southeast of the country (colored blue), which is more Catholic and has an older population. The northwest, on the other hand, is more liberal (orange). This divide is not new; Poland has been voting along the former pre-World War I borders for decades.

## Men and younger people vote more to the right

Although the two candidates received almost the same number of votes, there are differences: Men preferred to vote for the former boxer and soccer hooligan Nawrocki. He was 10 percentage points ahead among this group of voters.

> [embed: q-tool (chart/map) 8fcbd28255e890417dc9a282f5eae092]

Among older voters, Trzaskowski and Nawrocki were roughly neck and neck. The younger ones, however, had a clear favorite: Nawrocki, the future president. However, Nawrocki was not the first choice among young men. In the first round, most of them voted for the far-right politician Sławomir Mentzen , who came in third but failed to make it to the runoff.

> [embed: q-tool (chart/map) 596f09f1501e5776fb09e4901bfea993]

This is consistent with developments in other countries: across Europe younger voters, especially male voters, are increasingly turning to parties to the right of the political center. In the German federal election in February, the Alternative for Germany (AfD) became the second strongest party among voters under 25 .
